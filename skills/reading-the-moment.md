# Reading the moment

A tool feels made for a group when it starts where their conversation already is. The chat has
done half the work: people named options, said what they can do, argued about money, called dibs.
A generic tool throws that away and asks them to type it all again. Read first, then build.

## What to pull out

Answer these in a few words each, citing the message each fact came from.

1. **The job.** One verb and one object: "split the pizza order", "pick a dinner spot", "seat 16
   people at 2 tables". Not "help with plans".
2. **The decision shape.** Is the group choosing one thing, dividing things up, matching people to
   slots, keeping score over time, playing, or remembering? This decides the tool more than any
   keyword in the ask.
3. **People and roles.** Who is in the chat, who asked, who holds the money or the tickets, who
   has a car, who is the host. A role named in the chat ("i put it on my card") becomes a field.
4. **Content with sources.** Every option, item, amount, time and place, quoted from its message.
   "Noodle bar (Sasha: cheap but always a line)". Keep the colour: the wait, the price,
   the groan about the line. It belongs on the card as a detail line, not as banter.
5. **Constraints already stated.** "not the taco place again", "jun doesn't eat meat", "anywhere
   but by the door". These pre-fill the tool so nobody has to repeat themselves.
6. **What is missing.** The fact the tool needs that nobody said: the tip, the second car's
   seats, one person's share. Name it so the design can leave a clear place to fill it in.
7. **Tone and stakes.** Low-stakes and playful (who does dishes), practical (rides at 6 am),
   money (a $1,500 Airbnb), sentimental (a semester recap). Stakes set how loud the picture can be.
8. **Surface.** A Messenger chat card is small and lives between bubbles. An Event module sits
   under Details and speaks to everyone invited. A Group post serves people who barely know each
   other. Marketplace is a seller and strangers.

## Why each one matters

- The decision shape stops the most common miss: building a poll for a dividing-up problem.
- Roles make permissions real: only the person who paid edits the total; only the seller picks.
- Sources keep the tool honest. If you cannot point to a message, the fact is invented.
- "Missing" is where weaker builds go wrong: they make up a tip or a fourth option.

## Worked example: "muse split the pizza"

Chat: Sasha pasted "large pepperoni $22, half veggie $11, garlic knots $7, 2L soda $4, delivery
and tip $9"; Jun "i don't eat meat so not the pepperoni"; Ollie "i literally only had knots"; Bo
"i had the soda mostly".

- Job: split one pizza order fairly by what people ate.
- Shape: dividing up (items to people), not choosing.
- People: Sasha pasted the order, so Sasha paid. Four in the chat.
- Content: five lines with amounts, all from Sasha's message.
- Constraints: Jun is off the pepperoni; Ollie is only on the knots; Bo is on the soda.
- Missing: who shares the veggie half, how delivery is shared. Leave these as taps, not guesses.
- Tone: friendly, small money. Surface: chat card.

The tool that follows is a receipt where each line carries the faces of who shares it, already
set where the chat said so, and a per-person total that updates as people tap lines. Not a
generic "add items and amounts" form.

## Worked example: "muse who's being who"

Chat: group costume is Mario Kart; Bo has a green hoodie so Luigi; Sasha wants Peach; Ollie says
"i am not being toad"; Jun jokes about going as a banana peel.

- Shape: matching people to a fixed cast, one each.
- Content: Mario, Luigi, Peach, Yoshi, Toad (the known cast); the banana peel as a joke extra the
  group can add if they want.
- Constraints: Bo on Luigi and Sasha on Peach are wishes. Pre-fill them as "asked for" marks, or
  as claims only if the person clearly said "I'll be". Ollie "not Toad" is a soft block on his row.
- Missing: nothing critical. The tool is a cast list with five character cards.

## When the chat gives almost nothing

"muse count my pancakes, i can do 12" at a pancake breakfast: the content is one person, one
number to beat and a stake. That is enough. Do not pad it with extra features. A small, exact
tool beats a big empty one. See `filling-from-chat.md` for what to do when even the options are
missing.
